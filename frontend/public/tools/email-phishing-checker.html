What it checks

  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC results in the headers, and whether they align with the visible sender
  • From / Reply-To / Return-Path mismatches and Message-ID domain mismatches
  • CEO-fraud and BEC patterns: wire-transfer urgency, secrecy, gift cards, payroll and bank-detail changes
  • Login forms embedded in the email body and credential-harvesting language
  • Missing Received chain and other signs of hand-injected mail
  • Hidden text, tracking pixels and mismatched link destinations

How it works

The analyser parses the RFC 5322 headers and body locally in the request — nothing is stored. Authentication findings are deterministic (they come from the headers themselves); content findings name the exact phrases and structures that fired, so you can judge the evidence yourself.

What the result means

  • Failed or misaligned authentication plus a payment request is the classic BEC combination — verify by phone on a known number.
  • A clean authentication result does not make an email honest: a compromised real account passes every check. Judge the request, not just the headers.
  • Urgency plus secrecy ('don't call, just pay') is itself a red flag, whatever the score.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get the full email headers?

In Gmail: open the message, click the three dots, 'Show original'. In Outlook: File → Properties → Internet headers. Paste the whole thing, headers and body.

What is CEO fraud / BEC?

Business email compromise: a message impersonating an executive or supplier that asks for a wire transfer, a bank-detail change, or payroll diversion. It is the costliest email crime category reported to the FBI.

Is my email stored?

No. Analysis happens in the request and the content is not saved. Signed-in users can save the result summary to their history — the email itself is never kept.
