Email Tracking Pixel Checker

Many marketing and sales emails hide a 1×1 invisible image that phones home the moment you open the message — revealing your time, place and device. Paste an email and see exactly which trackers are inside.

Run the free check

Free, no sign-up. Nothing you paste is stored unless you create an account and save it yourself.

What it checks

  • Invisible 1×1 and off-screen remote images
  • Known email-tracking services and their per-recipient IDs
  • Remote images generally — the mechanism every open-tracker relies on
  • Hidden preheader text and other concealed content
  • Links whose visible text differs from their real destination

How it works

The analyser parses the email's HTML for remote resources and matches image signatures and hosts against known tracker patterns. It runs on the pasted content only; Aegis never loads the images, so checking cannot trigger the tracker.

What the result means

  • A single tracker in a newsletter is common; in a personal-looking sales or 'just checking in' email it means your opens are being watched.
  • Blocking remote images in your mail app disables nearly all open tracking.
  • Tracking in a phishing email tells the sender the address is live — one more reason not to open suspicious mail.

Frequently asked questions

Is email tracking legal?

It varies by jurisdiction and consent; the EU and India's DPDP Act both constrain undisclosed tracking. What matters practically: it is invisible by design, and you can block it.

How do I stop it?

Turn off automatic image loading (Gmail: Settings → Images → Ask before displaying; Apple Mail: Privacy Protection), which breaks pixel tracking.

Does Aegis load the pixel when checking?

Never. The analysis is purely textual — loading it would defeat the purpose.
