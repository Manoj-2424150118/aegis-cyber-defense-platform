TLS Certificate Inspector

The padlock is a claim; this inspector verifies it. For any host, Aegis completes a real TLS handshake and examines the certificate — who issued it, when it expires, whether the chain and algorithms still meet modern baselines.

Run the free check

Free, no sign-up. Nothing you paste is stored unless you create an account and save it yourself.

What it checks

  • Expiry and days remaining, issuer and subject, hostname coverage (SANs)
  • Signature algorithm (SHA-1/MD5 flagged) and key strength (RSA < 2048, EC < 256 flagged)
  • Protocol versions offered — TLS 1.0/1.1 are obsolete
  • Chain completeness and trust-path problems
  • Certificate transparency and revocation-relevant basics

How it works

A real handshake from Aegis servers, with certificate facts parsed from the DER encoding itself. Detections are facts about the presented certificate, graded against current Mozilla/baseline requirements.

What the result means

  • An expired or name-mismatched certificate on a payment page: stop, do not proceed — whatever the site claims.
  • Weak signatures and small keys are tomorrow's forgery; site owners should reissue on modern profiles.
  • A valid certificate proves control of the domain, not honesty — phishing sites have padlocks too.

Frequently asked questions

Does a padlock mean a site is safe?

It means the connection is encrypted to whoever controls that domain. Scam sites get free certificates in minutes — pair this check with the link checker.

How often should certificates be checked?

Before expiry, always — automated monitoring matters because a lapsed cert takes a site down and trains users to click through warnings.

What is TLS 1.3 and should I require it?

The current protocol version: faster and with obsolete cryptography removed. Supporting 1.2 + 1.3 and disabling 1.0/1.1 is the modern baseline.
