Domain Security Checker

Can a stranger send email as your domain? Should you trust mail from theirs? This check reads a domain's public DNS posture — the records that decide whether spoofing is trivial or blocked — and grades it.

Run the free check

Free, no sign-up. Nothing you paste is stored unless you create an account and save it yourself.

What it checks

  • SPF presence, syntax and the RFC 7208 ten-lookup limit that silently breaks it
  • DKIM selectors and key length (weak 1024-bit keys flagged)
  • DMARC policy strength: none vs quarantine vs reject, partial rollout (pct) and subdomain gaps
  • Dangling CNAMEs pointing at unclaimed services — a subdomain-takeover risk
  • MX, CAA and DNSSEC posture, and how recently the domain was registered (RDAP)

How it works

Everything here is public DNS and registry data, queried live. Posture findings carry no risk points against the domain's grade for being absent — the grade reflects how the records are configured, and each finding includes the concrete record to publish or fix.

What the result means

  • p=reject DMARC with aligned SPF/DKIM means the domain is genuinely hard to spoof.
  • No DMARC (or p=none forever) means anyone can send as that domain and most receivers will deliver it.
  • A domain registered days ago that emails you about payments deserves extreme suspicion.

Frequently asked questions

What is DMARC in one line?

A DNS record that tells receivers what to do with mail that claims to be from your domain but fails authentication — ignore, quarantine, or reject it.

My domain has no DKIM result — is it broken?

The checker probes common selectors; a negative means 'not detected', not 'proven absent'. Check your mail provider's settings for the real selector.

Can I check a domain I don't own?

Yes — these are public records. Checking your bank's, employer's or a suspicious sender's domain is a legitimate trust signal.
