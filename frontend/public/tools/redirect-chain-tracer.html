Redirect Chain Tracer

Short links and 'you won a prize' URLs rarely land where they claim. The tracer follows every hop — shortener, tracker, open redirect — and scores the page you would actually end up on.

What it checks

  • Every hop in the chain, with status codes and hosts
  • Click-tracking parameters (40+ known trackers) and the cleaned, tracker-free link
  • Tracking redirect wrappers used by mail and ad platforms
  • The final destination, scored with the full phishing-link engine
  • Chains that bounce through unrelated or newly registered domains

How it works

Aegis fetches the chain from its own servers with a guarded fetcher: every hop is re-validated, private and loopback addresses are refused, and only the redirect metadata is kept. Your browser never touches the suspicious site.

What the result means

  • A short, same-brand chain ending on the expected domain is normal for marketing mail.
  • Long chains crossing unrelated domains, or ending on a look-alike host, are a strong phishing pattern.
  • The cleaned link is the same destination with trackers stripped — share that one instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to trace a malicious link?

Yes — the fetching happens on Aegis servers with SSRF protections, not in your browser. You see the destination without visiting it.

Why do legitimate emails redirect so much?

Marketing platforms route clicks through their own domains to measure opens and clicks. The tracer separates that normal tracking from chains that end somewhere unexpected.

What is an open redirect?

A trusted site that forwards visitors to any URL given in a parameter. Attackers abuse it so the first hop looks like a brand you trust while the final page is theirs.
